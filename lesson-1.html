Formation, Geography & Administration

Jharkhand was carved out of the southern districts of undivided Bihar on 15 November 2000, becoming India's 28th state. The date coincides with the birth anniversary of Birsa Munda, the tribal freedom fighter from the Munda community.

The state spans the Chota Nagpur Plateau and Santhal Pargana region. Per the Government of Jharkhand's official state portal, the state is organised into 5 administrative divisions comprising 24 districts.

★ Key Insight

Jharkhand's rural population share (75.95%, Census 2011) is significantly above the national rural average. This matters directly for education delivery — most schools serve dispersed rural habitations, which is a recurring operational theme across every module in this chapter.

Module Takeaways

✓  Census 2011 population: ~3.30 crore, over 75% rural.

✓  Overall literacy 66.41%; a persistent gender gap exists (male 76.84% vs female 55.42%).

✓  ST population share (26.21%) is among the highest of any Indian state — this defines much of Jharkhand's education policy focus, covered in Module 3.

NITI Aayog Aspirational Districts Programme

Several Jharkhand districts are included in NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme — a national initiative that identifies districts lagging on health, education, agriculture, financial inclusion, and infrastructure indicators for concentrated, monitored improvement. Source: niti.gov.in/aspirational-districts-programme

! Why This Matters for Education Work

A state with such significant natural resource wealth having a large share of its districts flagged as developmentally "aspirational" is the central paradox that motivates Jharkhand's education reform agenda, discussed throughout Modules 2 to 5.

Module Takeaways

✓  Jharkhand ranks first in India for coal reserves and holds substantial iron ore, bauxite, and copper deposits.

✓  Several districts are part of NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme, reflecting a gap between resource wealth and human development indicators.

✓  This gap is the policy backdrop for the education reforms covered in the rest of this chapter.
